Right now, your stomach is trying to digest itself. That's not a metaphor. Every second, it pumps out hydrochloric acid and protein-shredding enzymes, the same chemicals that turn a steak into soup. And your stomach wall is made of the exact same stuff as that steak. So why aren't you dissolving? A microscopic defense crew. Specialized cells coat the lining in a thick shield of mucus, laced with bicarbonate that neutralizes the acid before it can touch you. But the shield isn't perfect. Acid keeps seeping through, killing cells on the front line. So your body runs a brutal replacement program. The cells lining your stomach are torn down and rebuilt every few days. Day and night, you are growing a brand-new stomach lining, just so the old one can be destroyed. And when that shield fails, thanks to painkillers, or a corkscrew bacterium called Helicobacter pylori, the acid wins. It eats straight into the wall, carving open wounds we call ulcers. So remember: food isn't the only thing your stomach is trying to digest. It has been trying to eat you your entire life. You just rebuild faster than it can.
