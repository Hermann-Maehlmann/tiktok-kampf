Right now, your skeleton is being dissolved from the inside. That's not a metaphor. Giant cells called osteoclasts grab onto your bones, seal off a tiny patch, and flood it with hydrochloric acid. The mineral melts away. Then enzymes chew through whatever is left. And the creepy part? These bone eaters come from the same family as your white blood cells. Normally, it's a controlled demolition. Right behind them, builder cells called osteoblasts fill every hole with fresh bone. Millions of these crews work at once, all over your body. But the balance is fragile. Eat too little calcium, and your body raids your bones to keep your blood levels steady. Stop putting weight on them, and the eaters take over. Astronauts lose one to one and a half percent of their bone density every month in space. And with age, the builders slow down, until the holes stop being filled. That's osteoporosis. Here's the twist. About ten percent of your skeleton is replaced every year. You are not standing on the skeleton you were born with. Your body eats it, and builds it again.
